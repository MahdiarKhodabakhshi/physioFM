# From "A Small EEG Foundation Model" to "A Predictive Theory of When Forecasting-SSL Helps": An Adversarial Proposal Review

## TL;DR
- **The candidate direction is viable but only if reframed as a general, cross-modal *diagnostic + bound*, not an EEG model.** As an EEG foundation-model paper, PhysioFM-S will not clear ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR — LaBraM (ICLR 2024), CBraMod (ICLR 2025), EEGPT (NeurIPS 2024), and BIOT (NeurIPS 2023) already own that lane with larger raw-signal models. The genuinely open white space is a *raw-data, pre-pretraining* score that predicts how much forecasting/predictive-coding SSL will help, validated across modalities, with an information-theoretic bound.
- **The novelty survives adversarial checking, but narrowly.** The closest competitor is "Spectral Predictability as a Fast Reliability Indicator" (arXiv 2511.08884, Oliver Wang, Pengrui Quan, Kang Yang, Mani Srivastava, submitted 12 Nov 2025), which scores raw data before training — but to select *zero-shot foundation models*, not to predict *SSL pretraining uplift*. No existing work links a predictive-information / excess-entropy score to the *magnitude* of forecasting-SSL benefit, nor proves a "downstream error ≤ f(predictive information)" bound. That is the defensible contribution.
- **Realistic outcome: ~25-40% at a top venue if executed as a theory+diagnostic paper with ≥15 datasets across ≥5 modalities and a proven bound; ~10-15% if it stays an EEG study.** The strongest single asset the user already has — the leakage audit and the order-shuffle/smoothing-flip causal controls — should be foregrounded as the empirical spine, but they must be generalized beyond EEG.

## Key Findings

1. **The top-venue bar for EEG/biosignal foundation models is now "big model, many datasets, SOTA."** LaBraM (ICLR 2024 spotlight; pretrained on exactly 2,534.78 hours of EEG across ~20 datasets, with a released base checkpoint of 5,822,770 parameters, using vector-quantized neural spectrum prediction + masked EEG modeling), CBraMod (ICLR 2025; pretrained on the ~9,000-hour, 19-channel TUEG corpus and evaluated on "up to 10 downstream BCI tasks (12 public datasets)"), EEGPT (NeurIPS 2024; a "novel 10-million-parameter pretrained transformer" using "a mask-based dual self-supervised learning method"), BIOT (NeurIPS 2023; cross-dataset tokenization), and Brant (NeurIPS 2023; intracranial). A 1-3M-parameter, 4-dataset DE-patch model cannot win on these axes — the user's self-assessment is correct.

2. **"When does time-series pretraining help" is a small, live, and mostly-empirical literature — no one has turned it into a predictive a-priori score.** The Netease Fuxi study "Examining the Effect of Pre-training on Time Series Classification" (arXiv 2309.05256; 150 datasets) concludes pretraining mainly helps models that *underfit* and that architecture matters more than pretraining-data volume. "United We Pretrain, Divided We Fail" (arXiv 2402.15404; XD-MixUp + SICC over 75 datasets) shows multi-dataset TS pretraining *can* work. Neither predicts, a priori, *which* dataset will benefit.

3. **All mainstream transferability estimators score a TRAINED model's features on target data — not raw data before pretraining.** LEEP (ICML 2020), LogME (ICML 2021), TransRate, Task2Vec, H-score, GBC, NCE, PACTran, and the representation-quality scores RankMe/LiDAR all require an existing encoder. This confirms the user's central distinction: scoring *raw data before any pretraining* is a genuinely different problem statement.

4. **The theory scaffolding exists but the specific bound does not.** Predictive information / excess entropy (Bialek-Nemenman-Tishby, Neural Computation 2001; Still, Entropy 2014) formalizes past-future mutual information. SSL linear-probe guarantees exist (HaoChen et al. spectral contrastive, NeurIPS 2021; Lee et al. "Predicting What You Already Know Helps," NeurIPS 2021; CPC/InfoNCE as a variational MI bound, van den Oord et al. 2018). But no result bounds downstream SSL error by a dataset's predictive information. That is open theoretical territory.

5. **The closest single threat to novelty is arXiv 2511.08884 (Spectral Predictability Ω).** It computes a data-only score before training — validated on "51 models and 28 datasets from the GIFT-Eval benchmark" — and finds that "large time series foundation models (TSFMs) systematically outperform lightweight task-trained baselines when Ω is high, while their advantage vanishes as Ω drops." Its target is *zero-shot foundation-model reliability / model-family selection*, not *SSL pretraining uplift*. The user must cite and distinguish it explicitly.

## Details

### A. Literature Landscape (five areas, cited)

**(1) EEG/biosignal foundation models & the acceptance bar.** The dominant recent top-venue EEG models are raw-signal, masked-reconstruction transformers scaled across many datasets: LaBraM (ICLR 2024 spotlight) uses vector-quantized neural spectrum prediction to build a neural tokenizer, then masked EEG modeling, pretrained on 2,534.78 hours across ~20 datasets. CBraMod (ICLR 2025) uses criss-cross attention and patch-based masked reconstruction on TUEG, evaluated on up to 10 downstream tasks across 12 datasets, claiming SOTA. EEGPT (NeurIPS 2024) is a 10M-parameter masked dual SSL model with spatio-temporal alignment, evaluated by linear probing. BIOT (NeurIPS 2023) tokenizes heterogeneous biosignals (EEG/ECG/HAR) into unified "sentences" for cross-data learning. Brant (NeurIPS 2023) targets intracranial signals. The common acceptance ingredients: large pretraining corpora, many downstream datasets, unified handling of channel/length mismatch, and SOTA or near-SOTA numbers. Note the field is beginning to critique these models (e.g., "Are Large Brainwave Foundation Models Capable Yet?", arXiv 2507.01196; "What Do EEG Foundation Models Capture?"), which is exactly the crack the user's rigor-first work can exploit — but a critique alone is a workshop paper unless paired with a general positive contribution.

**(2) Time-series SSL and "when does pretraining help."** Contrastive/predictive TS-SSL is mature: TS2Vec (AAAI 2022, hierarchical contrastive), TF-C / Time-Frequency Consistency (NeurIPS 2022, Zitnik Lab), TS-TCC, CoST, TimeSiam, and forecasting foundation models TimesFM (ICML 2024, Das et al., Google Research — "a single forecasting model pre-trained on a large time-series corpus of 100 billion real world time-points," "much smaller (200M parameters)") and Moirai/MOMENT. The characterization sub-literature is thin: arXiv 2309.05256 (pretraining helps underfitting models; architecture dominates), arXiv 2402.15404 (multi-dataset pretraining *can* work with the right interpolation), and cross-domain studies (UniTS, LPTM) that find some source domains transfer better than others. Crucially, these report *post-hoc* which datasets/domains benefited; none provide an a-priori, data-computable predictor of SSL benefit.

**(3) Predicting transferability a priori.** Two distinct families must be separated. (a) *Trained-model transferability estimators* — LEEP (ICML 2020), LogME (ICML 2021), TransRate, NCE, H-score, Task2Vec, GBC, PACTran — all embed target data through an existing pretrained model and score the features vs. labels. (b) *Representation-quality scores* — RankMe, LiDAR, intrinsic-dimension estimators — also require a trained encoder. The user's proposal belongs to neither: it scores *raw data or raw features before any pretraining*. The only works that score raw data pre-training are the forecastability metrics (below), and the dataset-alignment score CSI (arXiv 2502.11085, listed as TMLR 2026, Ghunaim/Hammoud/Ghanem, KAUST), which measures upstream↔downstream distributional distance (FID-like) and is objective-agnostic — but requires a candidate pretraining corpus and measures alignment, not intrinsic temporal predictability.

**(4) Theory of SSL & predictive information.** Predictive information I_pred = I(past; future) and excess entropy are classical (Bialek-Nemenman-Tishby, Neural Computation 2001, which defines I_pred as "the mutual information between the past and the future of a time series"; Still, Entropy 2014; past-future information bottleneck, Creutzig-Globerson-Tishby 2009). CPC (van den Oord et al. 2018) maximizes an InfoNCE lower bound on I(future; context). SSL linear-probe guarantees come from spectral contrastive theory (HaoChen et al., NeurIPS 2021) and conditional-independence-based reconstruction guarantees (Lee et al., "Predicting What You Already Know Helps," NeurIPS 2021). No published bound connects a dataset's predictive information magnitude to downstream SSL linear-probe error — the exact bound the user would need to prove.

**(5) Precedent "diagnostic/characterization/rethinking" papers at top venues.** The template that gets these in: introduce a simple, computable quantity; show it predicts or explains a widely-observed phenomenon; validate broadly with controlled ablations; and preferably back it with a bound. Examples of the genre: "Rethinking Pre-training and Self-training" (NeurIPS 2020); benchmark-leakage critiques that got traction on rigor; and data-property→transfer scores like CSI (TMLR 2026). In time series specifically, "Time Series Forecastability Measures" (arXiv 2507.13556, associated with KDD 2025) and Spectral Predictability Ω (arXiv 2511.08884) are the direct methodological ancestors of a data-only score.

**Where the user's finding sits.** The core empirical result — predictive-coding SSL helps in proportion to a task's temporal structure, with an order-shuffle control and a smoothing-flip natural experiment — is a *mechanistic* finding of exactly the kind the characterization genre rewards. But at present it is demonstrated on 4 EEG datasets and framed as an EEG study. Its natural home in the literature is between (2) "when does TS pretraining help" and (4) predictive-information theory — a position currently unoccupied.

### B. Novelty-Gap Analysis (adversarial)

**Claim under test:** *A raw-data, pre-pretraining "temporal learnability" score that predicts the magnitude of forecasting/predictive-coding SSL benefit, validated across modalities, with an information-theoretic bound.*

**Verdict: Novel, but narrowly — it survives only if you (i) target SSL *uplift* (not model selection), (ii) use a predictive-information formulation (not just spectral entropy), and (iii) actually prove a bound. The closest 5 prior works and how the user differs:**

1. **Spectral Predictability Ω — "…Fast Reliability Indicator for Time Series Forecasting Model Selection" (arXiv 2511.08884, preprint, submitted 12 Nov 2025).** *Closest competitor.* Computes a data-only frequency-concentration score before training and shows, over 51 models and 28 GIFT-Eval datasets, that large time-series foundation models beat lightweight models when Ω is high and lose that advantage when Ω is low. **Difference:** its target is *zero-shot foundation-model vs. lightweight-model selection*, not *how much SSL pretraining helps a given dataset*; and Ω is spectral concentration, whereas the user's construct is predictive information / k-step predictability gap. The user must cite it and differentiate on target (pretraining uplift vs. model choice) and on metric (predictive information vs. spectral entropy). **This is the most dangerous citation — a reviewer who knows it will accuse the user of a relabeling unless the distinction is sharp and empirically demonstrated (e.g., show Ω fails to predict SSL uplift where the user's score succeeds).**

2. **"Time Series Forecastability Measures" (arXiv 2507.13556, KDD 2025).** Proposes two data-only pre-modeling metrics (spectral predictability + largest Lyapunov exponent) that correlate with forecast error. **Difference:** predicts *forecast accuracy*, not *representation-learning transfer / SSL pretraining benefit for downstream classification*. The user's dependent variable (linear-probe uplift on a downstream task from forecasting-SSL) is different.

3. **CSI — "On the Importance of Pretraining Data Alignment for Atomic Property Prediction" (arXiv 2502.11085, TMLR 2026).** A data-property score (FID-like alignment between upstream and downstream datasets) that predicts transfer and is objective-agnostic (works for SSL denoising too). **Difference:** measures *upstream↔downstream alignment* and needs a candidate pretraining corpus; the user's score is *intrinsic within-dataset temporal predictability*, computed on the target dataset alone. Different construct, but the user must cite it as the strongest evidence that "data-property scores predict transfer" is a live, top-venue-adjacent idea.

4. **Lee, Lei, Saunshi, Zhuo — "Predicting What You Already Know Helps: Provable Self-Supervised Learning" (NeurIPS 2021).** Ties conditional-independence structure of data to downstream linear-probe sample complexity. **Difference:** it is a *sufficiency guarantee under CI assumptions*, not a computable a-priori score predicting uplift magnitude — and it is not about forecasting/temporal SSL. This is the closest *theoretical* relative and the natural launch point for the user's bound; the user's contribution would be a *quantitative, computable* predictive-information version, not a CI qualitative guarantee.

5. **Banville et al. — "Uncovering the structure of clinical EEG with self-supervised learning" (J. Neural Eng. 2021).** Introduces temporal shuffling as an SSL *pretext task* and adapts CPC to EEG. **Difference:** shuffling here is a training objective / ablation, not a *diagnostic score computed on raw data to predict SSL benefit across datasets*. The user's order-shuffle *control* is a validation device, and the framing "degree of raw-data temporal structure predicts SSL uplift" is unclaimed — but reviewers will note shuffle-based ablations are not mechanically new.

**What survives as defensible novelty:** (i) a raw-data conditional-mutual-information / excess-entropy score, validated with order-shuffle and smoothing-flip natural experiments, that *quantitatively predicts the magnitude* of forecasting/predictive-coding SSL uplift per dataset; and (ii) an information-theoretic bound of the form "downstream linear-probe error improvement from forecasting-SSL is controlled by the dataset's predictive information / excess entropy." Both are, to the best of this review's checking, unoccupied.

**What is NOT novel (state plainly):** structured DE patching, predictive-coding SSL for EEG (SleepDPC, IEEE 2021; ContraWR, JMIR AI 2023; Banville 2021), TimesFM adaptation, frozen-encoder linear probing, and the general observation that EEG benchmarks suffer subject/segment leakage. The leakage phenomenon is already documented: Brookshire, Kasper, Blauch et al., "Data leakage in deep learning studies of translational EEG," Front. Neurosci. 18:1373515 (2024, doi:10.3389/fnins.2024.1373515) report that segment-based holdout gave 99.8% [99.1-100.0%] accuracy but under subject-based holdout "test accuracy dropped to 53.0% [43.1-64.8%]... 95% confidence intervals that included chance performance of 50%" (Wilcoxon T=0.0, p=0.002); a related scoping review found "a 28-percentage point accuracy drop (from 94% to 66%) when proper validation was implemented" (PMC12468286). The user's leakage audit is therefore a *replication-plus-mechanism* contribution in EEG, not a first — it must be repositioned as one data point in a general predictability story.

### C. Ranked Candidate Contributions

**Contribution 1 (RECOMMENDED) — "Temporal Learnability predicts forecasting-SSL benefit": a data-only diagnostic + bound, validated across ≥5 modalities.**
- *Precise claim:* Define a computable score τ(D) on raw features of a dataset D (recommended: the k-step predictability gap = normalized reduction in next-window prediction error of a simple predictor over a persistence baseline, or equivalently an estimate of I(future; past | present)). Claim: τ(D) monotonically predicts the linear-probe accuracy gain from predictive-coding SSL over a no-pretraining baseline, across datasets and modalities, and this is bounded above/below by the dataset's predictive information.
- *Novelty vs. named work:* differs from Ω (2511.08884) by targeting SSL uplift not model selection; from CSI (2502.11085) by being intrinsic (no upstream corpus); from Lee et al. (NeurIPS 2021) by being a computable quantitative predictor with a bound rather than a CI sufficiency proof.
- *Experiments:* ≥15 datasets across EEG (Sleep-EDF, CHB-MIT, SEED, BCI-IV-2a), ECG (PTB-XL, PhysioNet), PPG/HAR (UCI-HAR, WISDM, Capture-24), audio (Speech Commands), and classic TS (UCR/UEA subset, traffic PEMS, weather). For each: compute τ(D) once on raw features; run predictive-coding SSL + frozen linear probe vs. no-pretraining baseline; regress uplift on τ. Controls: order-shuffle (destroys temporal τ, should null the uplift) and smoothing-flip (varies τ continuously). Ablations: τ variants (variance fraction vs. k-step gap vs. conditional MI), predictor family, patch size.
- *Theory needed:* a bound linking predictive information to linear-probe error reduction (Section D).
- *Key figure:* a single scatter — τ(D) on x-axis, measured SSL uplift on y-axis, ~15-20 points across modalities colored by modality, with the order-shuffle points collapsing to the origin and a fitted line + the theoretical bound as a shaded region. This one plot carries the paper.
- *Feasibility/risk:* High feasibility (the user already has the EEG pipeline, the controls, and the multi-seed/paired-test machinery). Main risk: the bound is hard, and the correlation could be modality-confounded.
- *ICML fit:* Strong, if the bound lands and the cross-modal scatter is clean.

**Contribution 2 — "Normalization destroys predictable structure": a formal account of why instance/RevIN normalization nulls forecasting-SSL benefit, with the smoothing-flip as the natural experiment.**
- *Precise claim:* Per-series instance normalization (and LDS smoothing) removes exactly the low-frequency / absolute-band-power components that carry predictive information; fixed corpus normalization preserves them. Formalize the information removed and predict, from τ, when instance-norm will hurt.
- *Novelty:* RevIN and instance norm are known to trade off distribution shift vs. information (the reversible-normalization literature); the novel part is tying the *removed* information to forecasting-SSL uplift via τ and demonstrating it with the smoothing-flip (0.1% vs. 17.2% within-sequence variance → null vs. large positive).
- *Experiments:* controlled normalization sweeps across the same multi-modal suite; measure τ before/after each normalization and the resulting uplift.
- *ICML fit:* Medium — strong as a section within Contribution 1, weaker as a standalone.

**Contribution 3 — "Leakage inflates SSL benchmarks in proportion to temporal autocorrelation": a general audit + predictor of leakage severity.**
- *Precise claim:* The inflation from segment-wise (vs. subject-wise / temporal-block) splits is itself predictable from τ(D): high temporal autocorrelation ⇒ large neighbor-leakage inflation. Turn the user's single EEG-SSL audit into a general law.
- *Novelty:* EEG leakage is documented (Brookshire et al., Front. Neurosci. 2024). The novel move is a *quantitative predictor* of leakage severity from the same τ, unifying it with the pretraining-benefit story.
- *ICML fit:* Medium; best as the "second act" of Contribution 1, strengthening the rigor narrative.

**Contribution 4 (weakest, do not lead with) — the EEG foundation model PhysioFM-S itself.**
- Even reframed, a small model on 4 datasets is a JBHI/AISTATS/workshop paper. Keep it as the *anchor case study* inside Contribution 1, not the headline.

**Ranking:** 1 ≫ 2 ≈ 3 ≫ 4. Contribution 1 is the paper; 2 and 3 are its most compelling internal sections; 4 is the reproducible anchor experiment.

### D. Recommended Direction + 6-Month Build Plan (Aug 2026 – Jan 2027)

**Pick Contribution 1**, absorbing 2 and 3 as sections and 4 as the anchor case study. Working title: *"Temporal Learnability: A Predictive Theory of When Self-Supervised Forecasting Helps."*

**Recommended diagnostic (be specific):** define the **k-step predictability gap**
  τ(D) = 1 − E[ε_model,k] / E[ε_persistence,k],
where ε_model,k is the k-step-ahead next-window prediction error of a cheap reference predictor (linear autoregressor or 1-layer GRU) on raw DE/feature windows, and ε_persistence,k is the error of the last-value baseline, averaged over horizons k and normalized per channel-band. Report the information-theoretic sibling **Î(future; past | present)** (a conditional-MI / excess-entropy estimate via a Gaussian or k-NN estimator) as the theory-facing quantity, and **within-sequence variance fraction** as the cheap proxy. Cross-validate that all three rank datasets consistently; lead with the k-step gap because it is task-aligned with the forecasting objective and cheap to compute.

**Theorem to attempt (state precisely enough to prove or falsify):** Let a forecasting-SSL encoder minimize multi-step next-window MSE. Under a linear-Gaussian latent-dynamics assumption (or the augmentation-graph formalism of HaoChen et al. adapted to temporal neighbors as positive pairs), prove:
  (downstream linear-probe error with SSL) − (error without) ≤ −c · g(I_pred(D)) + o(1),
i.e., the *improvement* from forecasting-SSL is lower-bounded by a monotone function g of the dataset's predictive information I_pred, and is zero when I_pred = 0 (the order-shuffle limit). Falsifiable prediction: shuffling time order sets I_pred→0 and must null the uplift; the smoothing-flip varies I_pred and must move the uplift monotonically. Even a bound under restrictive assumptions (linear-Gaussian) plus broad empirical validation is publishable; a fully general bound is a stretch goal.

**Datasets (≥15, ≥5 modalities):** EEG — Sleep-EDF, CHB-MIT, SEED, BCI-IV-2a (anchor, already done); ECG — PTB-XL, MIT-BIH/PhysioNet; PPG/wearable — a PPG HR dataset + Capture-24; HAR — UCI-HAR, WISDM, PAMAP2; audio — Speech Commands; general TS — a UCR/UEA classification subset, traffic (PEMS), weather/electricity. This spread is what converts "an EEG study" into "a theory."

**Month-by-month:**
- **Aug 2026:** Freeze the diagnostic definitions (τ, Î, variance fraction); build a single unified pipeline (feature extraction → τ computation → predictive-coding SSL → frozen linear probe → uplift) that runs identically on every modality. Reproduce the 4 EEG anchor results inside it.
- **Sep 2026:** Add ECG + HAR (5-6 datasets). Run the full τ→uplift regression on ~8 datasets; check the scatter is forming. Run order-shuffle and smoothing-flip controls on each.
- **Oct 2026:** Add audio + general TS + traffic/weather to reach ≥15 datasets across ≥5 modalities. Lock multi-seed protocol, subject-wise/temporal-block splits (to avoid the leakage the user already exposed), and paired significance tests.
- **Nov 2026:** Prove the bound under the linear-Gaussian / temporal-augmentation-graph assumption; verify the empirical scatter respects the theoretical shaded region. Do the ablations (τ variants, predictor family, patch size, normalization sweep = Contribution 2).
- **Dec 2026:** Turn the EEG-SSL leakage audit into the general "leakage severity ∝ τ" result (Contribution 3). Write the paper; build the carrying figure. Internal red-team against Ω (2511.08884), CSI, and Lee et al.
- **Jan 2027:** Polish, stress-test the bound's assumptions, add a "when our score fails" honesty section (spatial-spectral tasks like SEED/BCI-IV-2a as the low-τ regime), finalize, submit (~late Jan 2027 ICML deadline).

**The experiments that carry the paper:** (1) the cross-modal τ→uplift scatter with the theoretical bound overlaid; (2) the order-shuffle collapse and smoothing-flip monotonicity as causal natural experiments; (3) a head-to-head showing Ω / a trained-model transferability estimator do *not* predict SSL uplift as well as τ.

### E. Honest Risk Assessment

**Top 3 rejection paths and de-risking:**

1. **"This is Spectral Predictability Ω / forecastability metrics relabeled for SSL" (novelty).** De-risk: include a direct experiment where Ω and forecast-error metrics fail to predict SSL *uplift* while τ succeeds; make the "uplift vs. model selection" and "predictive information vs. spectral concentration" distinctions explicit and early. If τ turns out to correlate ~perfectly with Ω empirically, the paper collapses — check this in September as a go/no-go.

2. **"The bound is trivial / assumptions do the work" (theory).** De-risk: prove the cleanest non-trivial version (linear-Gaussian or augmentation-graph), state assumptions honestly, and lean on the falsifiable predictions (shuffle→0, smoothing-flip monotone) so the theory is empirically anchored even if the general case is open. A bound that merely restates CPC's InfoNCE MI bound will be seen as derivative — it must say something about *downstream* error, not just the pretext objective.

3. **"Correlation is modality-confounded / it's just 15 dots" (empirical strength).** De-risk: within-modality replication (multiple datasets per modality so the trend holds *inside* a modality, not just across), leave-one-modality-out prediction of uplift, and per-dataset paired significance. Reviewers will count datasets — 15 is a floor, 20+ is safer.

**Realistic acceptance probability.** As a theory+diagnostic paper with a proven (even if assumption-laden) bound, ≥15 datasets / ≥5 modalities, the causal controls, and the Ω/transferability head-to-head: roughly 25-40% at ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR — a real but not safe shot, typical for a strong analysis paper. If the bound does not materialize, it drops to a rigorous empirical characterization paper (~15-25%, better at NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks or as an ICLR borderline). If it stays EEG-only, it is not an ICML paper (~10%) and should go to JBHI, AISTATS, or an ICML/NeurIPS workshop.

**Best alternative framing if ICML is not realistic:** submit the multi-modal diagnostic (without a full bound) to **NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks** or **AISTATS** (which values the statistical-diagnostic angle), and place the EEG anchor + leakage audit at a **JBHI** or an **ICML/NeurIPS workshop** (e.g., SSL: Theory and Practice, or a time-series workshop) as a companion. The workshop route also lets the user plant a flag on the "temporal learnability" framing early while the full paper matures.

3. **Run the Ω-vs-τ go/no-go by end of September.** If τ merely reproduces spectral predictability, pivot to Contribution 2/3 or a benchmarks paper immediately.
